In airway epithelium, what does CFTR normally do?
Secretes Cl⁻ into the lumen and inhibits ENaC, limiting Na⁺ and water reabsorption.
Why is mucus dehydrated in cystic fibrosis?
↓ CFTR + ↑ ENaC → excess Na⁺ and H₂O absorption from airway lumen → thick mucus.
Why is sweat salty in cystic fibrosis?
Sweat ducts fail to reabsorb Cl⁻ via CFTR; Na⁺ reabsorption also falls → ↑ sweat NaCl.
Why can sarcoidosis cause hypercalcemia?
Activated granuloma macrophages express 1α-hydroxylase → ↑ 1,25-(OH)₂ vitamin D → ↑ Ca²⁺ absorption.
Dynein transports cargo in which direction?
Retrograde, toward the microtubule − end/centrosome. Mnemonic: Dynein draws back.
Kinesin transports cargo in which direction?
Anterograde, toward the microtubule + end and cell periphery.
What protein defect causes primary ciliary dyskinesia?
Axonemal dynein dysfunction → impaired ciliary motility; may cause situs inversus and infertility.
What is the Gs signaling pathway?
Gs → ↑ adenylyl cyclase → ↑ cAMP → ↑ PKA.
What is the Gi signaling pathway?
Gi → ↓ adenylyl cyclase → ↓ cAMP → ↓ PKA.
What is the Gq signaling pathway?
Gq → PLC → PIP₂ cleavage → IP₃ + DAG; IP₃ ↑ Ca²⁺ and DAG activates PKC.
What mediates type I hypersensitivity?
IgE cross-linking on mast cells → immediate degranulation.
What mediates type II hypersensitivity?
IgG or IgM against cell-surface or extracellular-matrix antigens.
What mediates type III hypersensitivity?
Circulating antigen-antibody immune complexes deposit in tissues and activate complement.
What mediates type IV hypersensitivity?
T cells; delayed reaction via Th1/Th17 inflammation or CD8⁺ cytotoxicity.
Lambert-Eaton: where is the defect?
Presynaptic voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels → ↓ acetylcholine release; strength improves with use.
Myasthenia gravis: where is the defect?
Postsynaptic nicotinic ACh receptors (or MuSK); weakness worsens with repeated use.
Why can dopamine antagonists cause parkinsonism?
D₂ blockade in the nigrostriatal pathway reduces dopaminergic facilitation of movement.
Which dopamine receptor does pramipexole mainly target?
D₂/D₃ agonist, with particularly high affinity for D₃ receptors.
Ketamine is pharmacologically similar to what dissociative drug?
PCP; both are noncompetitive NMDA receptor antagonists.
What causes prolonged apnea after succinylcholine in some patients?
Butyrylcholinesterase (pseudocholinesterase) deficiency → impaired succinylcholine metabolism.
How does dantrolene treat malignant hyperthermia?
Blocks RyR1-mediated Ca²⁺ release from skeletal-muscle sarcoplasmic reticulum.
Which pathway explains antipsychotic extrapyramidal symptoms?
D₂ blockade in the nigrostriatal pathway.
Why can bromocriptine help neuroleptic malignant syndrome?
It is a dopamine agonist that counteracts severe dopamine blockade.
How does sacubitril increase natriuretic peptides?
Inhibits neprilysin → ↓ breakdown of ANP/BNP → natriuresis and vasodilation.
What are major effects of ANP and BNP?
↑ Natriuresis/diuresis, vasodilation; ↓ renin, aldosterone, and blood pressure.
Class IA antiarrhythmics: key action?
Moderate Na⁺ blockade + K⁺ blockade → ↑ AP duration and QT. Quinidine, procainamide, disopyramide.
Class IB antiarrhythmics: key action?
Weak Na⁺ blockade → ↓ AP duration, especially in ischemic ventricular tissue. Lidocaine, mexiletine.
Class IC antiarrhythmics: key action?
Strong Na⁺ blockade → marked conduction slowing with little AP-duration change. Flecainide, propafenone.
What happens to the HCM murmur with decreased preload?
It gets louder: smaller LV volume worsens dynamic LV outflow obstruction.
Typical serum and urine findings in SIADH?
↓ Serum Na⁺/osmolality with inappropriately concentrated urine and ↑ urine Na⁺.
What is vasopressin resistance?
Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus: kidneys fail to respond adequately to ADH.
Which disease causes impaired intestinal copper absorption?
Menkes disease (ATP7A) → low copper and ceruloplasmin; kinky hair and neurodegeneration.
Why does copper deficiency affect connective tissue?
Copper is a cofactor for lysyl oxidase, required for collagen and elastin cross-linking.
Which classic diseases use CAG repeats?
Huntington disease and spinocerebellar ataxias; CAG encodes polyglutamine.
Which classic disease uses a CTG repeat?
Myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DMPK).
Missense vs nonsense mutation?
Missense changes one amino acid; nonsense creates a premature stop codon.
Motor neuropathy plus alopecia suggests what rare disorder?
Consider Brown-Vialetto–Van Laere spectrum or other syndromic motor neuropathies; phenotype matters.
What causes cavitation in pulmonary tuberculosis?
T-cell–driven granulomatous inflammation and macrophage enzymes cause caseous tissue destruction.
What organism causes trachoma?
Chlamydia trachomatis serovars A, B, Ba, and C.
What does the 3rd aortic arch form?
Common carotids and proximal internal carotid arteries.
What does the left 4th aortic arch form?
Part of the aortic arch between the left common carotid and left subclavian arteries.
What does the 6th aortic arch form?
Proximal pulmonary arteries; left distal portion forms the ductus arteriosus.
Through which foramen does CN VII leave the skull?
Stylomastoid foramen, after traversing the internal acoustic meatus and facial canal.
What does myeloperoxidase produce in neutrophils?
HOCl from H₂O₂ + Cl⁻ in phagolysosomes; a potent microbicidal oxidant.
What do matrix metalloproteinases do?
Zn²⁺-dependent enzymes that degrade extracellular-matrix proteins during remodeling.
