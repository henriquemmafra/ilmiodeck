Cystic Fibrosis: Airway Pathogens and Culture Media

Study classic age-related colonization patterns and high-yield culture clues. The curves are illustrative, not current epidemiologic estimates.

Visual memory clues · Gram stain, culture and identification

Pseudomonas aeruginosa

Gram-negative rod · Oxidase-positive · Non-lactose fermenter on MacConkey agar

Grapes = grape-like odor; blue-green pigment = pyocyanin. The green pigment is NOT the Gram stain or a requirement for MacConkey growth.
Staphylococcus aureus

Gram-positive cocci in grape-like clusters · Mannitol salt agar

Purple clusters + yellow agar from mannitol fermentation.
Haemophilus influenzae

Gram-negative coccobacillus · Chocolate agar · Requires factor X (hemin) and V (NAD⁺)

Chocolate bar marked X + V = chocolate agar and growth factors.
Burkholderia cepacia complex

Gram-negative rods · B. cepacia selective agar

Selective agar + important opportunistic pathogen in cystic fibrosis.

Clinical reminder: multiple organisms can coexist, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa can be acquired in childhood.
